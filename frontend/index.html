External Case Study

A regional competitor entered via mass retail at half our price point and captured 12% share in one year. Our board wants to match their price. What is the real strategic choice being avoided here?

Thinking Depth Rating

🟡 Level 2

Critique

You correctly frame this as a pricing decision, but stop short of naming the underlying trade-off: matching price abandons the brand equity that justifies your premium in the first place, and signals to every other segment that quality was never the real differentiator.

Pushing Thinking Deeper

Reframe the choice: is 12% share worth conceding the category-leader narrative permanently? Consider which customer segment the competitor is actually serving — is it one you were ever positioned to defend?

Hidden Resolution — tagged case_study_resolution, excluded from retrieval until now

What Actually Happened

Blazar held price and instead re-launched its salon channel around a "clinically tested, dermatologist-endorsed" claim set, ceding the price-sensitive segment entirely. Share loss stabilized at 8% after two quarters, but average realized price rose 6% and salon repeat-purchase rate improved — the board's fear of losing share was real, but matching price would have permanently traded away the one asset the competitor couldn't copy.
